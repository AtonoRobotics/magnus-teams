# Connecting an app grants its use: one screen, one per-app card, descriptions screened

Spec rev 140, "Permissions and tasks", replaces every earlier approval rule. Its first part: connecting an app shows one screen, and accepting it lets Magnus use every tool the app offers, with no review of individual tools. The owner: "per-tool pin reviews become the connect-time consent screen, plus one per-app card when a third-party server adds a write tool; descriptions are screened, not shown to me." This replaces the tool-definition reviews of DECISIONS.md (lines 53, 122, 192 and 937) and the review half of `2026-11-04`'s brokered pin entry.

**The connect screen.** `GET /v1/connectors/{name}/terms` words it from the app's classes: what Magnus can read, what it can change and send (undoable, not undoable, money), and the risks (what it reads may try to steer Magnus; who what it sends can reach; what cannot be undone; payments ask every time; whether Alpha Vector reviewed it and how its updates come). A catalog connector's classes are its signed manifest's. A third-party server's come from the tools it lists, classed by its own hints (read-only hint: read; not destructive: undoable; otherwise not undoable). The desktop app shows it before any sign-in; `POST /v1/connectors/{name}/consent` with the screen's `terms` hash records the consent (`tools` event, `consented`), and a screen that no longer matches is refused (409), so consent always covers what was shown. Accepting classes a third-party server's tools; where it could not list them before the sign-in, they are classed when the sign-in completes. Reason: the spec's one screen, and consent bound to the words the person read.

**Catalog updates.** A brokered connector's definitions come from the image its manifest names by digest, so a new definition there is the next release's and is accepted with it (`release`). Nothing asks.

**The per-app card.** When a third-party server (added by its address, not the catalog's, not the config file's) lists a tool that is not classed: one that only reads is classed and used under the consent; ones that change or send raise one `app_tools` card for the app, to the owner, naming what they do in plain words. Keep classes them as the card said; Disconnect removes the app for everyone, revoking every sign-in. Never one card per tool, and no second card while one is open. The tools wait until it is answered. Reason: the spec's "asks once, for the whole app, on one card: keep it or disconnect". It goes to the owner because the classes are the organisation's and Disconnect removes the app for everyone.

**Description screening.** Every app tool definition the server has not seen is screened before the agent sees it (`tool_gate.rs`): its description and every description and title in its inputs, by the fixed injection phrases and then the model that screens inbound content (`[safety] inbound_model`, else `monitor_model`), with the same prompt. A failed screen holds the tool back; nothing else does. The person never sees a description. Each screen is a `tools` event (`screened`), listed in the developer view (Policy, Tool screens; `GET /v1/dev/tool-screens`) and in the run inspector of the run whose listing found it; a model screen's reply is the run's `reviewer_reply`, purpose `tool_screen`.

When no model can judge it: a tool's first definition is offered on the phrases alone, as inbound text is (`2026-11-09-safety-model-checks.md`), because the person accepted the app as listed when they connected. A definition that changes after that is the rug pull B10 exists for, so it is held until a model passes it or the person connects the app again, which accepts the app as it is. Reason: the spec's screen assumes one ran; holding an unjudged change is the safer reading of "only a failed screen holds the tool back", and it keeps invariant 1 (`a_changed_tool_definition_is_disabled_and_hidden`) true unchanged, so no invariant patch is needed.

**Deleted.** The `tool_review` and `tool_change` inbox items and their decisions, the kernel's `review_item` ("Review the tool descriptions of …"), `run/pins.rs`, the owner's tool table with descriptions and per-tool selects in Settings › Connectors, the two cards in the app, the admin console's "Tools not reviewed" status, and the descriptions in the owner's tool listing. The classify endpoint stays as the owner's override in the API.

**Kept readers.** `tool_pinned` and `tool_changed` are never edited: a pinned definition the owner approved stays shown, one never reviewed is screened at its next listing, and a changed one is held until screened. An old `tool_review` or `tool_change` item reads as resolved (`superseded`, by the kernel), so it waits on no one.
