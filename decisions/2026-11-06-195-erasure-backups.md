# A person's erasure is incomplete while a backup still holds their content (2026-11-06)

- A backup the scrub cannot read or rewrite no longer stops the others or goes unrecorded: the scrub goes on to the rest, records the backups left as `BackupScrubPending` (the files, why, and which removals they have not had: after `from`, through `through`) and fails; a later scrub that leaves none records `BackupsScrubbed`. Recorded once per change, not at each try. Reason: `erase_person` logged a failed scrub and answered as if the person were erased, while the backup kept their content, against "deleted from the store and from every backup" (#195; test `an_erasure_a_backup_cannot_take_stays_incomplete_until_the_backup_is_rewritten`).
- The scrub is tried again at start (as before, for a removal cut short) and on the trash's hourly round while one is pending. Reason: the kernel already scrubs at start and checks the trash hourly in `trash::schedule`; a second timer would only duplicate it, and the record in the log makes the pending cleanup survive a restart and replay (same test: pending after a restart, done after the next start once the directory is writable).
- The erasure is reported incomplete until then: the erase answer says `erasure: incomplete` with the backups left (and `complete` otherwise), the console's toast says so, the owner's Settings › Data and privacy › Backups says how many deleted people's data and backups are still held and why, and the audit has `user_erasure_incomplete` and later `user_erasure_completed`, from the kernel, under the person's provider subject as the console's `user_deleted` names them. Reason: the owner and the admin must not read a deletion as complete while a backup holds it (same test).
- A backup that could not be rewritten is not deleted or refused: a restore leaves out the content of every event a removal named, from a record of the removals (event numbers and times only) kept in the state directory and beside the backups (`removals.json`), written by each scrub before it rewrites any backup. Reason: the restore runs on an empty log, so the removals are not in it; the state directory is the kernel's own and writable when the backup disk is not, and the copy beside the backups goes with them to another machine. Restoring such a backup brings the person's records back without their content, as restoring a backup from before a trash removal does (test `a_backup_left_holding_an_erased_persons_content_does_not_restore_it`).
- Not changed: a backup that is corrupt stays pending until it is pruned after `keep_days` or the owner deletes it; Magnus does not delete a backup by itself. Reason: deleting the owner's backups is the owner's call.
- A backups directory that is missing while backups were made in it or a scrub is pending (a disk not mounted), or that cannot be listed, is out of reach, not empty: the scrub keeps the pending backups pending (or, with none pending, records the directory as pending for the latest removal) and fails, instead of recording `BackupsScrubbed`. A directory never made, with no backup ever made, is still one without backups. Reason: the missing-directory branch and `list()` (which turned a `read_dir` failure into no files) gave the scrub nothing left, so it called the erasure complete and stopped the retries while the backups out of reach still held the content (review of #243; test `an_erasure_stays_incomplete_while_the_backups_directory_is_out_of_reach`).
- `removals.json` is only added to, never replaced: each scrub and each restore reads the records in the state directory and beside the backups, adds the removals in the log, and writes the whole set back (a record that cannot be read is left as it is, and stops a restore). Each removed event is recorded by its number and its time, and the scrub and the restore leave out the content of every event so recorded, in every backup, not only the removals after the backup's own. Reason: after a restore of a backup older than an erasure, the restored log lacks that erasure, so rewriting the record from the log forgot it and a later restore of a backup the erasure could not rewrite brought the person's content back; the time keeps an event the restored log later gives the same number from being taken for the removed one (review of #243; test `a_restore_of_an_older_backup_does_not_forget_a_later_erasure`).
