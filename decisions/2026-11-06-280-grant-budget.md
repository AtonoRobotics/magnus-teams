# A grant's budget holds across its tasks and through deletion (#280, review of #278)

- A grant keeps its own record of what its tasks' model calls cost, each charge with its time (`Grant.charges`, charged in the projection as each model reply, verifier reply, memory pass, spoken script, extraction or failed call is charged to the run). Its spend in a period is the charges within it. Reason: spend was summed over the tasks and runs still listed, so deleting a task under a grant gave its cost back and let new work spend it again. Charging by when the money was spent also makes a rolling period mean the cost in that window, not the cost of runs created in it. Test: `standing_authority.rs` `a_grants_budget_holds_across_its_tasks_and_deleting_one_gives_nothing_back` (deleted and removed for good, its spend stays).
- Every model call of a task under a grant first checks the grant's spend for the period, by all its tasks together; once it is spent the run stops with the reason `grant_budget` and a notice. Reason: each task started under a grant was given all of what was left then, and only checked its own budget before a model call, so tasks running at once could each spend the whole allowance. Test: the same (a task waiting on an approval while the grant's budget runs out stops at its next model call).
- The person may resume a run stopped for `grant_budget` (the notice's Continue, or the task's Resume); it then goes on under its own budget alone, and its approvals ask again (the grant no longer covers them while spent). Reason: going past the grant for one task is the person's own decision, and a stop they could not get past would leave the task stuck. "Double the budget" stays for the run's own budget only. Test: the same (resumed, it asks the model again and does not stop again).
- A model call already under way can still take the grant a little past its budget (one call per running task), as a run's own budget can (#273). Reason: the cost of a call is known only when it returns; bounding a request ahead of time is #273's.
