# A workspace signs in to an app with the team's own account

**Choice.** A Team Admin (a workspace's owner or admin) connects an app the workspace was granted, such as GitHub, for the workspace from its People page, under a new Apps section. The sign-in is the same OAuth flow a person uses from the Library. It is kept in the vault under the workspace's key, `connector_<app>__workspace:<id>` (`team::sign_in::owner_key`), never under a member's. A team-thread run's calls to an app people sign in to act through that sign-in (`Kernel::app_owner`): admission, the tool list, every call and the step boundary. Outside a team thread, and for an app with no sign-in, nothing changes. A member's own sign-in still never serves a team thread. A Team Admin also chooses who may use each app: nobody, Team Admins only, or members and admins. Disconnecting revokes the sign-in at the app and deletes it here; members' own connections are not touched.

The team agent is told every app: each granted app, the roles it is open to and whether it works now ("Connected for the workspace", or how a Team Admin connects it), and the server's other apps, which a Team Admin can grant. Before this the team agent saw only the granted apps' names. GitHub is not granted to any workspace on production, so the agent neither knew it existed nor had a way to it.

**Reason.** The spec: "An identity is one account at one site, owned by a person or a workspace. A vault scope is per person or workspace." "A member's personal connectors, computer and vault entries are never used for someone else's request." "An admin grants a team connector, such as a shared mailbox or CRM, to the workspace with rules per role. Members use it under their role." Until now an OAuth app could never work in a team thread at all: the only sign-in that existed was a person's. A workspace-owned one is what the spec describes. Keeping it under its own key keeps it apart from every person's, and a person id cannot contain `:` (`accounts::valid_person_id`), so one can never be read as the other.

**Not done.** A workspace sign-in opens no conversation when it completes, since a workspace has no Magnus chat of its own. The admin console's per-app list leaves workspace sign-ins out of its people and shows them with the workspace's grant (`signed_in`).

Tested in `team::sign_in::tests`. The earlier refusal, `team::privacy_tests::a_team_run_never_uses_a_members_own_sign_in`, still holds.
