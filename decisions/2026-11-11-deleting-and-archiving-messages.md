# Deleting a message, and deleting or archiving a conversation

The owner asked: "users should be allowed to delete the messages from their account or archive them."

The spec says "deletion means deletion" (Observability, audit, export and deletion), and for every other kind of content that deleting removes it from the live data at once and from backups within the 30-day purge window. It says nothing of deleting a chat message or conversation, so these follow the common team-chat practice (Slack, Teams, WhatsApp), with deletion meaning deletion.

- **A sender deletes their own message, for everyone.** Only its sender may, as only its sender may edit it. It travels as a new chat change, `unsent`, so no event kind is edited. Its text leaves the ledger at once, and the events that hold its text (the message and each edit of it) have their content taken out of the log through the usual removal (`Ev::Removed`, item `message:<id>`) and out of the backups by the scrub. To find those events the team ledger now notes, as it applies them, which event holds each message's text and edits (`Ledger::apply_at`). A message whose content is gone replays as nothing; `unsent` keeps its place, so replies still hang from it. The thread shows "This message was deleted" where it had replies, and nothing where it had none, as Slack does. Its reactions, pin and edited mark go with it, and the team agent no longer sees it in a channel's history.
- **Deleting a direct conversation is for the person who deletes it.** It empties the conversation for them and takes it off their list (`cleared`, up to now). The other people keep their copy: deleting it for them would be one member deleting another's record of their own conversation, which no rule gives them. When someone writes again, the conversation comes back with only what came after. Search, the activity view, unread counts and the pop-ups follow the same mark. A channel is the workspace's, so it cannot be deleted for one person: it can be put away.
- **Archiving puts a conversation or channel away, for its archiver only.** It moves to an Archived list at the foot of the rail, from which it is opened or brought back. It comes back by itself when someone else writes in it after it was archived, so a new message is never missed, as Gmail and Slack do; the person's own messages do not bring it back.

Not done: when every person in a direct conversation has deleted it, its content could be erased from the log as well. Nobody asked for that, and the team agent's runs in that conversation keep its history as their own record, so it is left until the spec speaks to it.
