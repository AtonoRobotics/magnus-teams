# Team privacy: projects, notes, private answers and sign-ins (spec rev 142)

A review against spec rev 142 found four places where team data reached people the spec keeps it from. Each fix follows the spec's own words.

**Project channels and files are their people's.** "Each project has its channels, files, a task board, its knowledge base and its members." `Workspace::sees` now checks project membership as well as a direct conversation's people. `sees_file` does the same for team-volume files. Every route that names a channel or a file goes through them: the workspace read, messages, the view, posting, whispers, notes, the panel, reading, deleting and moving files, and the agent's `team_read_file`. Before, only the home rail and search filtered. An outsider gets "no such channel" or "no such file", as if the thing did not exist. The kb routes already worked this way. A channel's members are now the people who can see it.

**Working notes are the thread's, and expire with the task.** "Visible to the members of that thread" and "expire with the task." The notes route now needs the channel to be one the caller sees. When a task ends, the kernel sweep (`expire_notes`) removes its notes' content from the log through the usual removal path: an `Ev::Removed` named `notes:<task>`, the content deleted, and the backups scrubbed. The ledger drops them too. No event kind is edited. A `Note` whose content is gone replays as nothing. The task's other team events keep their content.

**An answer built from the requester's own data is theirs.** "Data fetched under one member's entitlements … stays in that requester's run." "Data crosses from personal to team only by an explicit share." A run marked `private` (it read data only its requester may see) no longer has its final answer shown to the thread. The requester sees the answer, marked as theirs alone. Everyone else sees a line saying the agent answered them directly. The requester can post it themselves (`POST /v1/workspaces/{ws}/answers/{task}/share`), which is the explicit share, done as their own message, like sharing a whisper. This is the safer of the two readings. The other was to send the answer through Auto Review as if it were a `team_post`. That reading would put the agent's judgment in place of the requester's for data that is only theirs. The agent can still `team_post` mid-run, through the boundary as before. An answer that read nothing private is shown as before.

**A team run never uses a member's own sign-in.** "A member's personal connectors, computer and vault entries are never used for someone else's request." "An admin grants a team connector … to the workspace … Members use it under their role." Before, a granted app that each person signs in to ran on the requester's personal OAuth sign-in. Now such an app (`signs_in_per_person`: it has a credential and `auth = "oauth"`) is not offered to a team run, is not listed through the requester's sign-in, and is refused at the boundary. Granted apps with no credential, or with the organisation's key, work as before. The harness no longer lists the requester's watched apps in a team run.

**Left for later:** a sign-in held by the workspace itself, made by an admin and stored in the vault under a workspace key. Until it exists, a team connector that needs a per-person sign-in does not work in a team thread; the refusal says why. Building it touches the sign-in flow, the boundary's credential step, the destination read used by sends and the brokered generation, all keyed by person. That is a change of its own, and part of it is the send code that has just changed.
