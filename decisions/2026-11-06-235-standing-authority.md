# Standing authority (2026-11-06)

- A grant is a person's standing authority over a category of their own work: a title (the category, in words), the apps it covers (one or more of theirs), the exclusions they lifted, and a budget of model cost per period. Events: `grant_set` (an edit is a `grant_set` with the same id; the title is content, removed with the person's), `grant_revoked`, and `grant_used` for each action it covered. Grants are personal: `GET /v1/grants` lists the caller's own only, the owner's included, and `PUT`/`DELETE /v1/grants/{id}` answer 404 for anyone else's. Erasing a person removes their grants. Reason: behavior spec (Standing authority); the issue's spec-review line (grants created, edited, revoked and used are in the event log by construction). Test: `standing_authority.rs` `a_grant_is_its_persons_alone_and_revoked_in_settings_or_by_telling_magnus`.
- A grant comes only from the person, in two ways: "Handle things like this" on a task proposal (`decide_card` decision `handle`), or accepting a grant card Magnus put (`propose_grant`, a read like `propose_task`, chat turns only) when they asked in conversation. Approving a proposal never sets one, and the model never sets one by itself: it proposes, they accept (invariant 6). Handle starts from the proposal's title, the person's apps its evidence read, nothing lifted, a week, and four times its estimate (at most the appliance's limit for one task); the person changes any of these in the form before choosing it. It sets the grant and starts the proposal's task under it, once (its grant id is derived from the card, so a handle interrupted between the two finishes on the next try). Test: `handling_things_like_this_sets_a_grant_whose_tasks_go_ahead_and_approving_a_proposal_sets_none`; `a_grant_card_is_accepted_with_the_persons_changes_or_declined_with_a_reason`.
- A grant card is accepted, with the person's own changes to its fields, or declined with a reason (400 without one); it is not put off, as a proposal is not. Only the person it was put to answers it (404), once (409). A card with `replaces` changes that grant, keeping its id, while it is still theirs and live. Grant cards are in the suggestion ledger ("you proposed the grant"), so a declined one is not put again without what changed. The model's card is refused when an app is not one of the person's, an exclusion other than `send` or `irreversible` is lifted, the period is not day, week or month, or the budget is not above 0 and at most the appliance's limit. Test: the last test.
- The kernel writes the grant as Cedar from its fields (`policy::grant_cedar`) and stores the text with it, shown in the API and in Settings: `permit(principal == Person::"<p>", action == Action::"execute", resource) when { resource in App::"a" || … }` with forbids. Names are quoted as the policy layers quote them, so an app's name cannot become policy. Coverage evaluates that policy alone (`grant_covers`), as a layer is evaluated. Reason: the spec's "a grant is a Cedar policy in the person's layer"; a person never writes Cedar, and generating it from fields keeps every grant inside the shape the kernel checks. Test: `policy.rs` `a_grant_covers_its_apps_less_what_stays_excluded`.
- What still asks: spending money (`financial`) in every grant, and anything prohibited; what cannot be undone (`irreversible`) and sends (a change that reaches beyond the person: audience not private, risk not read or sandbox) unless the person lifted them. Spending money is not liftable, as it is not for a confirmed schedule: the kernel cannot measure an amount, so the spec's spend budget could not be enforced, and a limit that cannot hold is not offered. Deleting and committing the person to anything are one exclusion, "Delete or commit me to anything", because the kernel's one risk class `irreversible` cannot tell them apart. Test: `sends_and_what_cannot_be_undone_ask_until_lifted_and_money_and_the_rule_of_two_always_ask`.
- A grant covers an action only in a task started under it (`create_task` with `grant`, or the proposal it was handled from), and only where the organisation's policy asked: the approval stage, right after a confirmed schedule's coverage and in the same shape, when no approval covers the action, the ask is the policy's (the kernel's own asks, the Rule of Two and the audience rule, are never covered), the grant is its run's person's and not revoked, that person could approve this risk themselves (the owner, `approver:<risk>`, or a reversible write), its period's spend is under its budget, and its policy permits the action. A Deny from the organisation's policy never reaches the question. Each covered action is a decision "covered by the grant G" and a `grant_used` event, listed under the grant (latest first) with its task. Reason: owner's ruling, 2026-11-02 (only over their own work, only what they could approve, within the organisation's policy); "Magnus reports every action taken under one". Test: the first two tests; `inv6_policy.rs` `the_agent_proposes_a_grant_and_only_its_person_grants_what_they_could_approve` (carol, no approver role: an irreversible send under her grant still asks).
- The budget is model cost per rolling period: a day, 7 days or 30 days back from now, the sum of the cost of the runs of tasks started under the grant within it. A task is refused under a grant whose budget for the period is spent (a tool error, no task), and a task started under one may spend at most what is left (its budget is the appliance's, its `max_usd` the smaller of the two). Rolling periods need no calendar or time zone and cannot be gamed at a boundary. Test: `a_grant_whose_budget_for_the_period_is_spent_starts_no_task`.
- Magnus knows the person's live grants in a conversation: the system context of a chat turn lists each one live when the turn started (id, title, apps, what still asks, its budget per period), so it starts matching work with `grant`. The budget left is not in it: it changes as tasks spend, and the context stays the same at every call of a turn; the kernel refuses a task under a spent grant instead. A title whose words came from a run that read outside content is not shown, as the ledger does for such a card. Revoking one at the person's word (`revoke_grant`, their own only) narrows what Magnus may do, so the model may do it on request. Test: the first and fourth tests.
- In the app: the task proposal card has "Handle things like this", opening the grant form in place (the title, the person's apps as checkboxes, "Send on my behalf" and "Delete or commit me to anything" as checkboxes, "Spending money always asks", the period and the budget); a grant card shows the same form with Accept and Decline (with a reason). Settings, Data and privacy, has "What Magnus may do on its own" beside the relationship notes: each grant with its apps, what still asks, its spend this period, the actions taken under it, its policy, and Edit and Revoke. Not tested in the installed app here (no installed-app run for this change).

The agent can propose policy and grants, never grant them." The test file's header carries it, and one test is added; the existing tests are unchanged.
