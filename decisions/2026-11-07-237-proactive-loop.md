# The proactive loop is reviews, inbox items, and gates (2026-11-07)

- Reviews are non-chat background tasks the kernel starts, one per person at a time (#237). Reason: all five review outcomes work without chat — nothing (complete), observe/interrupt (new run records), propose (the existing task-safe `propose_task`/`propose_schedule`), act (the run's own tools within its grant, the gate asking when it has none). A chat turn per review would flood the conversation list, and `create_task`'s chat-only path is not needed.
- Observations and interrupts are inbox items (`observation`, `interrupt`), dismissed/accepted through the existing respond path. Reason: items already post, list, resolve, instrument (delivered/opened/dismissed/response time fall out of item events), erase with the person, and show in the app — a parallel queue would rebuild all of it. Schema validation for observation cards is the `observe` record's required what/why/evidence.
- Quiet hours delay attention, not the post: items carry `details.notify_at`, and Needs-you counts only what is due. Reason: there is no push channel; the item existing is the post, its counting for attention is the notification. Over-budget and out-of-hours interrupts downgrade to observations (visible next session), which is what "wait" means.
- Hours run in the person's `timezone` preference (IANA, chrono-tz, default UTC). Reason: working hours are meaningless without it, and the crate is already vendored.
- Event wakes cover task done/failed, new approvals, coming-up schedules and due commitments. New app items need a connector push primitive that does not exist, so they are their own issue. The app-home "what changed" surface and the Cedar suggest/interrupt/act actions are their own issues: each is one concern with its own tests, and one #237 pull request holding all of it would be unreviewable.
- Reviews are runs, not chat turns ("Magnus's review" was the abandoned alternative). Reason: the issue says "wakes a review run", and a turn per review would flood the conversation list; all five outcomes work without chat.
- Wake marks land at wake time (`ReviewWoken` sets `reviews_last`, the overnight day and the schedule/commitment/approval marks), not at completion. Reason: a review that fails must not wake again on the next sweep; with marks at wake, the loop degrades to one attempt per interval even when every review fails.
- A review completes without a task contract. Reason: its work is the observations it queued, already in the log — there is nothing to check a contract against, and the person-review fallback for unchecked criteria would ping the person after every review. Non-review runs are refused exactly as before.
- An out-of-hours interruption queues as an observation whose `notify_at` is the next working-hours start; a budget-spent one uses quiet-end-or-now. Reason: `notify_at` is the only attention signal (Needs-you hides the future), so "waits for the next session" must be a future timestamp, not now.
- Ended reviews and chat turns never wake reviews. Reason: a review waking a review is a loop, and a chat turn's person is already there.
- The review's pushed context is unfinished work, schedules, approvals, disagreements and commitments; the suggestion ledger, relationship notes and recent messages arrive through the standard person section every run gets. Reason: no duplication of what the prompt already carries; library and connected data stay pullable within the wake budget.
- E2E tests trigger coming-up reviews rather than waiting for cadence. Reason: the sweep's first tick runs before the harness's "listening" line, so preferences set after boot cannot shape the first review; a schedule created after the preferences lands guarantees the triggered review sees them.
- The review completion and observe/interrupt handlers are boxed (`Box::pin`), not inline. Reason: their futures sit on the stack below Cedar policy evaluation, which refuses to run with under 100KB left; growing `record_call`/`complete` starved the verifier's reads with a "recursion limit" denial (found via #370 CI).
- The boxing above is reverted: #375 grows the stack at policy evaluation itself, so caller depth no longer matters. Reason: the systemic fix supersedes the workaround; the merge ref stays green without it (validated before pushing the revert).
