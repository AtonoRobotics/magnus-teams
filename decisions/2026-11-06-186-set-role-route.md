# The kernel's role cannot reach the log through SET ROLE (2026-11-06)

- `migrate` checks every role the kernel's role is a member of, directly or through roles in between, inherited or not, and itself, as if the kernel were that role (`pg_has_role(..., 'MEMBER')`). Reason: a NOINHERIT role holds nothing through a membership until it switches to it with SET ROLE, so checking only the role's own privileges let a role made beforehand in `pg_write_all_data` pass and then change the log (#186). Test: `migrate_refuses_a_kernel_role_that_can_set_role_to_change_the_log` (log_review.rs).
- Such a membership is refused, naming the role and what it allows, when that role can UPDATE, DELETE or TRUNCATE `events`, holds an attribute `migrate` strips from the kernel's role (SUPERUSER, CREATEDB, CREATEROLE, REPLICATION, BYPASSRLS), or is one of `pg_read_server_files`, `pg_write_server_files`, `pg_execute_server_program`. Reason: a role the kernel can become must not do what the kernel may not, and Postgres documents those three as a way to superuser. Test: `migrate_refuses_a_kernel_role_that_can_set_role_to_change_the_log` (log_review.rs).
- Refused, not revoked. Reason: `migrate` already refuses an inherited membership that can change the log and only resets attributes, which are the role's own; a membership was given by someone on purpose, and taking it away is theirs to decide. Test: `migrate_leaves_the_kernel_role_unable_to_change_the_log` (log_review.rs) still expects the refusal.
