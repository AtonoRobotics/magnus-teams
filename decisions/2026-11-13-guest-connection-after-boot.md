# A computer's guest connection: the CONNECT line in one write, and a bounded reopen before the request

**Cause.** Firecracker reads the `CONNECT <port>` line of a host connection to the VM's vsock socket once, without waiting, as soon as the stream has any bytes (`read_local_stream_port`, a nonblocking `read_exact`), and drops the stream if the line is not all there. computerd's relay wrote the line with `writeln!` on an unbuffered socket, which is three writes: `CONNECT `, `52`, `\n`. When Firecracker woke between them, it dropped the stream: the relay saw "Connection reset by peer" (the rest of the line arrived unread) or "Broken pipe", answered `{"ok":false}`, and the kernel's or the test harness's request failed. A loaded host, as just after a VM boots, makes the gap likelier. The guest agent was listening all along; the attachment sandbox (`sandbox.rs`) and the brokered servers already sent the line in one write and never failed this way.

**Evidence.** Three invariant tests run five times each on Strix Beta before the change: 6 of 15 failed, every one with "Connection reset by peer" or "Broken pipe" on a guest connection (inv1 1/5, inv2 2/5, inv3 3/5). The new relay test shows the old line reached the socket as `[CONNECT ][52][\n]`. After the change, ten runs each: 27 of 30 passed, and no run had a reset or a broken pipe. The three failures were all in round 1, while other jobs filled the shared test Postgres ("remaining connection slots are reserved"; the server not healthy in 30 s). None of them reached a computer.

**Choice.**
- The relay sends the line in one `write_all` (`relay::send_connect`), tested to reach the socket as one write.
- The kernel's guest client (`computer::client::guest_for`) opens the connection again, at most `GUEST_CONNECT_TRIES` (3) times `GUEST_CONNECT_RETRY_WAIT` (250 ms) apart, when the relay did not reach the guest. Nothing has been sent then, so this is safe for every request. Firecracker also drops a host connection a guest has not taken within two seconds (`CONN_REQUEST_TIMEOUT_MS`), which a guest busy just after boot can miss. Once the request is written it is never sent again, whatever it was: a reset after that is the caller's error, as before.

**Not done.** No retry after the request is sent, even for reads: the client does not know which requests are idempotent, and the guest may have run a write or an `exec`. The guest agent is unchanged.
