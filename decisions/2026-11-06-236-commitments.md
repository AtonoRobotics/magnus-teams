# The commitment ledger (2026-11-06)

- A commitment is what the person or Magnus said they would do: `who` (`person` or `magnus`), `what`, `to` (for whom, optional), `by` (a date `YYYY-MM-DD`, due at its end in UTC, or an RFC 3339 time, optional), where it was said (`source`: the conversation, with its thread and task, or an app, with the tool and the call it was read in), the `quote` it was recognised from, and its status (`open`, `done`, `released`). Events: `commitment_recorded`, `commitment_changed` (a correction or a status change, by the person or by Magnus, with an optional note) and `commitment_removed`; what, to, quote and note are content (`events::CONTENT`), in the person's wall and erased with them. Erasing a person removes their commitments from the state too. The issue's instrumentation (recorded, corrected by the person, completed) is these events by construction; raised is the proactive loop's (below). Reason: behavior spec (Commitment recall); #236. Test: `commitments.rs` `a_commitment_said_in_conversation_is_recorded_and_carried_into_every_later_run`.
- Magnus records one with `record_commitment` (app `memory`, as `relationship_note`: a chat turn and a task may use it, it needs no contract, and it changes nothing outside the appliance), only where its words were really said: the quote, trimmed and at least 8 characters, must be in the person's own words of the run (their message, not an opener's instruction; the conversation it carries; a task's text when they gave it, not a schedule's), or, for Magnus's own, also in its replies in the conversation and the run; or in the result of a call the run read that it names as `call` (a read of an app or the web, not an error, not the appliance's chat or memory tools). The match is verbatim but for case and spacing, and a result's JSON is searched as its strings, so an escaped line break does not hide a quote. Anything else is refused, and nothing is recorded. Reason: the spec review's "Magnus records none that were not made"; a model can paraphrase a wish into a promise, but it cannot make the kernel find words that were never said. Test: `only_words_really_said_or_read_are_recorded_and_never_twice` (a mail's words without the read named, and words no one said, are refused).
- A commitment already open for the person with the same `what` (any case or spacing) is refused, naming its id. Its id is derived from the call's idempotency key, so a call made again after a crash records it once. The check and the record are made under one lock (`RECORDING`), so two runs recording the same commitment at once record it once. Test: the second test.
- Overdue is derived, never stored: open with its date past. The API derives it at each read; a run's context derives it as of when the run started, so its context is the same at every call (C1). A done or released one is never overdue. Test: `overdue_is_derived_and_done_or_released_ones_leave_the_context`; `commitments::tests::a_date_is_due_at_its_end_and_a_time_when_it_says`.
- Every run of the person, chat turn or task, carries their commitments open when it started, as they stood then (the steps of each before the run's `task_created`), in its system context under "# Commitments", oldest first, each with its id, who, what, for whom, by when and OVERDUE, within the same 16KB as the other sections. One recognised in content read outside the conversation is marked as data, not instructions. The kernel's own passes carry none, as they carry no relationship memory. Reason: the issue's "in context for the review and the wakes it bears on" (#237 reads the same section). Tests: the first and third tests.
- Taint: a run starts with the combined taint of the person's open commitments, joined at `task_created` as the playbook's items are, so words read in a mailbox (untrusted and private) do not enter a clean run untainted (M1, I2). A commitment's taint is that of the run that recorded it, and a person's correction does not clear it: the words they kept may still be the mail's. Done, released or removed, it leaves the context and its taint with it. Test: `a_commitment_read_in_untrusted_mail_taints_each_run_that_carries_it`.
- Magnus marks one of the run's person's open commitments done or released with `update_commitment` (with a note how it learned it); another person's is refused as no open commitment of this person's. Test: the third and fourth tests.
- The ledger is private: `GET /v1/commitments` lists the caller's own only, and `PUT` and `DELETE /v1/commitments/{id}` answer 404 for anyone else's, the owner and admins included. No admin or developer view reads them: they are no run's events and no bundle document. The intent of a `record_commitment` call is in its run's trace, as a `relationship_note`'s is. Reason: "private to them", as relationship memory (#232). Test: `the_person_corrects_completes_and_removes_theirs_and_no_one_else_sees_them`.
- The person corrects what, for whom and by when, sets the status (open again included), and removes one. Removing it deletes the content of its events with a `removed` event, from the backups too, as a removal from the trash does. Test: the fourth test.
- Raising an overdue commitment (pushing it to the person, "overdue on open") is the proactive loop's, #237; here it is only the derived flag in the API, the context and Settings.
- In the app: Settings, Data and privacy, "What you and Magnus said you'd do", after "What Magnus may do on its own": each commitment with who, what, for whom, by when, Overdue, where it was said and its quote, with Done, Release (or Reopen), Edit and Remove. Not tested in the installed app here (no installed-app run for this change).
